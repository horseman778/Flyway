**Full version:**

```
Play alone right now: enter a name, tap Practice, and two bot observers join your watch. They are good but not perfect, so trust but verify.

Flyway: The Great Count is a cooperative counting game for 2–8 players. It is dawn at a coastal marsh and the migration is passing through. Each player guards one watchpost and sees only their own sector. Neighboring sectors overlap, so the same bird can appear in two notebooks.

Each round, everyone tallies herons, avocets and terns, marks how many sit in the shared strip, and posts their notebook to a live Team Sheet. A rotating Lead Counter then files the team's report: the true total for each species, plus the exact cell of the one rare spoonbill. The reveal shows the whole marsh, rings every bird that was double counted and marks the decoys, so you can see exactly where the team went wrong.

Five rounds of about two minutes each. The marsh gets busier as you go, and decoy egrets and dunlins appear from round 3.

Why it matters: avoiding double counting when views overlap is the core problem of real citizen-science bird counts. Flyway turns it into a team puzzle that works in classrooms, families, nature clubs and team-building sessions.

Details:
• Join from any phone or laptop with a four-letter code. No login, no install.
• A "Your move" bar tells each player what to do right now, for their role and phase.
• "I'm done scouting" ends the round early once every observer is ready.
• Rejoin after a dropped connection; if the Lead leaves, the role hands over automatically.
• A personal life list of six species, field ranks from Novice Birder to Keeper of the Flyway, eight field badges and a shareable result card.
```

## Quick start for a judge (about 3 minutes)

1. Open the link, type a name, tap **Practice**.
2. Scout: count the target birds in your sector, then how many of them sit in the hatched shared strip. Tap **SHARE TO TEAM SHEET**, then **I'M DONE SCOUTING**.
3. Lock in: you are the Lead in practice. For each species, add up everyone's SEEN and subtract half of everyone's OVERLAP (those birds were counted twice). Mark the spoonbill's cell and tap **FILE THE REPORT**.
4. Reveal: watch the double-counted birds get ringed and see your score.
